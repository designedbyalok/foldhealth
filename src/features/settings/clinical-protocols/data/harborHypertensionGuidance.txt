1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This guidance standardizes blood pressure (BP) assessment, treatment and follow-up for adults managed by Harbor Health care teams.
Applies to: adults 18 years and older with essential hypertension (ICD-10 I10) or hypertensive chronic kidney disease (ICD-10 I12.9) enrolled in Disease Management or the Special Needs Plan care program.
Exclusions: pregnancy or the first 12 weeks postpartum (follow HH-CPG-OB-002); patients under 18; hospice or comfort-focused care; secondary hypertension managed by a specialist.

2. MEASURING BLOOD PRESSURE
Use a validated, upper-arm automated device with the correct cuff size. Seat the patient for 5 minutes, back supported, feet flat. Base decisions on the average of at least 2 readings taken on at least 2 occasions. Home readings count when taken with a validated device: 2 readings 1 minute apart, morning and evening.

3. BP CATEGORIES AND RESPONSE
Normal: systolic below 120 and diastolic below 80 mmHg. Reinforce healthy lifestyle; recheck yearly.
Elevated: systolic 120-129 and diastolic below 80 mmHg. Lifestyle changes; recheck in 3 to 6 months.
Stage 1: systolic 130-139 or diastolic 80-89 mmHg. Lifestyle changes for all. Start medication when 10-year ASCVD risk is 10% or higher, or the patient has diabetes or chronic kidney disease.
Stage 2: systolic 140 or higher, or diastolic 90 or higher. Start medication. When BP is more than 20/10 mmHg above goal, start two first-line agents from different classes.
Severe elevation: systolic 180 or higher, or diastolic 120 or higher.
 - Without symptoms: contact the treating provider the same day; recheck within 1 week.
 - With chest pain, shortness of breath, new weakness or numbness, confusion or vision change: direct the patient to call 911 or go to the emergency department.
Low BP: systolic below 100 mmHg, or dizziness or fainting on standing. Do not increase any antihypertensive; provider review within 1 business day.

4. BP GOALS
Most adults, including those with diabetes or chronic kidney disease: below 130/80 mmHg.
Age 65 and older with frailty, falls or orthostatic hypotension: systolic below 140 mmHg, individualized by the provider.

5. MEDICATION TITRATION PATHWAY
Confirm adherence and home technique before every step. Allow 4 weeks between steps unless BP is in the severe range.
Step 1: Start lisinopril 10 mg once daily (ACE inhibitor). If ACE inhibitor cough, use losartan 50 mg once daily (ARB). For Black patients without chronic kidney disease or albuminuria, start amlodipine 5 mg once daily or chlorthalidone 12.5 mg once daily instead.
Step 2: Not at goal after 4 weeks: increase lisinopril to 20 mg, then to 40 mg once daily (losartan to 100 mg).
Step 3: Still not at goal: add amlodipine 5 mg once daily; increase to 10 mg after 4 weeks.
Step 4: Still not at goal: add chlorthalidone 12.5 mg once daily (or hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg once daily).
Step 5: Above goal on three agents including a diuretic at maximum tolerated doses (resistant hypertension): add spironolactone 25 mg once daily if potassium is below 4.5 mmol/L and eGFR is above 45 mL/min/1.73m2, and refer to nephrology or a hypertension specialist.
Never combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB. Stop ACE inhibitors and ARBs if angioedema occurs.

6. LABORATORY MONITORING
Basic metabolic panel (potassium, creatinine, eGFR) at baseline and 1 to 2 weeks after starting or increasing an ACE inhibitor, ARB, diuretic or spironolactone.
Potassium above 5.5 mmol/L, or creatinine up more than 30% from baseline: hold the new or increased agent, repeat the panel within 72 hours and notify the provider.
Potassium below 3.5 mmol/L on a thiazide-type diuretic: notify the provider for replacement.
Every 12 months: basic metabolic panel, lipid panel, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, and HbA1c for patients with diabetes.

7. CARE MANAGEMENT CHECK-IN SCHEDULE
Not at goal, or a medication change in the last 30 days: care manager check-in every 2 weeks with a home BP log review, until at goal at 2 consecutive check-ins.
At goal: monthly check-ins for 3 months, then every 3 months.
Before each provider visit: home BP twice daily (morning and evening) for 7 days.
Provider visits every 4 weeks during titration; every 3 to 6 months once BP is controlled.
Review medication adherence at every check-in. Proportion of days covered below 80% triggers a pharmacist referral.

8. ESCALATION
Two consecutive check-ins with an average home systolic BP 10 mmHg or more above goal despite confirmed adherence: provider review within 5 business days.
Any reading in the severe range: follow section 3.
